This interactive tool, developed by the Justice Connection, helps showcase trends in case filing and clearance by the U.S. Department of Justice' Legal Information Office Network System (LIONS). All data is from publicly released DOJ data, which ranges from October 1994 to July 2026 and will be regularly updated on a monthly basis with information from LIONS. For more information about this data, see the footnote below.

About this data: The DOJ National Caseload data dashboard is sourced and cleaned from monthly filings from the Department of Justice’s Legal Information Office Network System (LIONS). LIONS data, also known as the DOJ Case Management System (CMS), is aggregated into a redacted annual fiscal year report at the close of the year. However, monthly data is provided in an 88-gigabyte file available to the public, with some categories and identifying information redacted. This dashboard provides users a way to make data actionable and understood over time from 1994 to 2026. Justice Connection does not manipulate the data or conduct any analysis of the data; this dashboard is a data management tool that allows users to investigate the data as its presented in the DOJ’s distribution. Data validation is possible by comparing the DOJ’s annual filings to monthly data downloads. However, given the manual input processes of the LIONS system and lack of information due to redaction or sealed cases, it’s important to note that all data cannot be independently validated. LIONS data updates monthly. Monthly updates include revised data from previous months, meaning updates may differ from previously posted numbers. Based on data analysis, Justice Connection estimates that a near-complete picture of monthly filings can be assumed after a three-month lag. In other words, for the most accurate monthly filing data, users should only analyze data from three months prior, because more cases data will be added in the subsequent two updates. Terminations take longer than filings to settle - six months or more - so the provisional marking on termination and disposition series covers a wider window than on filings. For more information about the LIONS system, please visit the DOJ’s website. Note not all DOJ cases are reflected in this data set because the system is used by U.S. attorneys offices (USAO), meaning it will exclude cases from Main Justice. Some inquiries are not logged into the system if they are considered “obviously trivial,” as well. Most information in the LIONS system is obtained during case intake and screening stages, according to the DOJ’s website. Any additional information is filed as the matter moves through proceedings.
